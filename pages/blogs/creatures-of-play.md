## creatures of play

by: owen stegall
date: 04/07/26

"on april 7th, 2026, people stopped being born. on the same day, people stopped dying, and people stopped aging." 

i'm reading jon bois's *17776: what football will look like in the future* for what is likely the fourth time today. i can't remember if [this novella][17776] introduced to me his work, and i don't even know if it's my favorite work of his, but its themes are more poignant than ever right now. 

### how do you spend an infinite amount of something?

if you haven't heard of jon bois, 17776 is a great introduction. it's as an [award-winning][award] story that centers around a world wherein no one is born, ages--except for children, who grew into adults--or dies, and nano-technology saves everyone from environmental dangers. essentially, all problems have been solved. yet, the american people who are gifted eternal life spend most of it playing football.

however, they don't play normal games of football. instead, they play in one of the hundreds of thousands of football games that are happening simultaneously and have their own unique rules. for example, the first of many games discussed is one where the entire football field is the state of nebraska, with it's eastern and western borders acting as endzones. while seemingly pointless in nature, there are still people who wish to play, including a character named nancy, who is seen running into a tornado to avoid would-be tacklers. 

these absurd games are viewed and presented by anthropromorphic space probes. the aforementioned quote referencing today's date is from a sentient version of the real-life [pioneer 9][pioneer-9] (nine). nine is attempting to explain this new world to the recently conscious [pioneer 10][pioneer-10] probe (ten). ten thinks that people are wasting their time playing football and doesn't understand why people aren't exploring and conquering new land like they've always done, such as by going into space. in response, nine states that these people evolved past a need to explore and conquer; rather, they are now "creatures of play." in the absence of problems to solve or places to go, these silly football games provide them an avenue to create and tell meaningful stories.

for example, nancy is flung by the tornado to the town of bee, nebraska. she is temporarily housed by a local, who talks about his personal experiences with football and informs nancy as to how a ballroom down the street was made entirely of concrete. nancy responds by saying that she didn't know where the tornado would throw her, but she knew that there would be something interesting. she also notes that "every little square of [america], every place you stomp your foot, that's where something happened. something wild, maybe something nobody knows about, but something." she considers herself as merely a bookmark running across the country through all of these stories, to which the local says, "seems okay to me."

humans have an innate desire to have their life have mean something. even after thousands of years of existence, nancy imagines a nation where there is no space left for her story to be told. in a life with infinite time, nancy can only think of stories as taking up a finite amount of space in the united states. and yet, the local thinks that the country still has something left to give, because those stories are still meaningful. when nancy comes to bee, nebraska, she is allowing others to tell the stories of their life, which gives them more meaning.

storytelling is same reason that i've loved watching sports for my entire life. sure, the actual action of the sport is fun to watch, but the underlying story makes it all the better. we love to root for the underdog, not because they play the sport well, but because they tell a great story. we need to recognize the importance of each person's individual life-story because it brings us together and gives those life-stories meaning. unfortunately, it seems that this tenet of holding all people in equal regard has degraded in american political and cultural society, which has subsequently affected people across the world.

### what the (nuclear) football will look like in the future

on easter sunday, the president of the united states, donald trump, [threatened iran][easter-post] by stating, "open the fuckin' strait [of hormuz], you crazy bastards, or you'll be living in hell - just watch! praise be to allah." this morning, [the president doubled down][today-post] and said "a whole civilization will die tonight" at 8pm est if he does not get his way. 

these intimidation tactics originate from [iran shutting down the strait of hormuz][iran-hormuz], which itself started because the united states and israel launched missiles that [killed iranian government leaders][supreme-leader-missles]] as well as [innocent schoolgirls][school-missles]. the administration has attempted to justify the strikes by using [several different bases][iran-war-basis], including [stifling the nation's nuclear proliferation][iran-war-nuclear]. nonetheless, there is now [speculation that the united states itself may be launching nuclear weapons][us-nuclear] today based on the president's comments.

while i doubt that a nuclear missile will be launched today, the fact that someone could even make this inference based on the president's remarks is absolutely shocking. further, countless people have been impacted as a result of the united states's unnecessary military actions in iran. american gas prices are at its [highest][high-gas] in years, [asian nations have significantly altered their lives][asia-iran-war], and thousands in [lebanon, iran, and other nearby nations have been displaced, injured, or killed][iran-war-casualties].

at first, i could not understand how the united states was able to do something so harmful. then, i realized that this administration does not view all people as equal. the lives and stories of people in iran mean nothing to trump, as evidenced by his [threats to commit war crimes][iran-war-crime-threats]. trump has recently [called somali immigrants][trump-somali] "garbage" from "shithole countries", and he has thinks immigrants in general][trump-immigrants-animals] are "animals." trump and his administration are able to do inconceivable things to people in the middle east because they don't see them as actual people with personal lives and unique stories. trump's message this morning that threatens to destroy an entire nation of people just reaffirms their incapacity to care about other people. 

although i don't have the ability to change the opinions of america's political leaders, this country still voted trump into power, even though he has [been extensively documented as a xenophobe][trump-racist]. thus, his voters likely expected, and maybe even wanted, him to take drastic measures against people who are different from them. american society no longer sees other people as fellow human beings, but as members of out-groups. this country needs to come to an understanding that all people have their own lives and stories that deserve respect. as 17776 posits, the first step is to evolve past a desire to conquer and explore, and instead focus on being creatures of play, like juice.

### juice and lunchables

i would be remiss if i discussed 17776 without talking about the jupiter icy moons explorer (abbreviated as juice). juice is another character-[satellite][juice] that gets involved in storytelling from time to time. juice is the personification of a creature of play because he has a sense of wonder that has alwayas fascinated me. 

converse to nancy's beliefs, juice provides specific places in the country that, even after thousands of years, have not been walked across. this is a concept that has stuck with me since the first time i read it. think about it. there probably is a patch of grass or forest somewhere in america where no one has stood. however, juice doesn't contextualize this unexplored land as something that needs to be conquered; he just enjoys knowing that it exists. although, he does think that children would've wandered onto the land, just to do it. to me, that makes the joy and wonder of children as a great real-world example of creatures of play.

sports also provide juice an outlet for understanding how to be a creature of play. as he puts it, "i still get fucked up about it, the idea that play is the point of existence now. and i cant really wrap myself around that, so i find a game to watch." i'm probably going to continue to use sports in a similar manner, where i can watch them and feel a sense of community around watching the same story as everyone else. we're all just watching people play, and that's enough. 

i'm going to see the local baseball team, [the columbia fireflies][fireflies], this thursday, and hopefully that can help with my current existentialism. in summation, i'm mainly writing this just to explore the different emotions and thoughts i've been grappling with instead of doomscrolling for hours on end waiting for 8pm to hit. plus, i think writing helps me take different facts and apply them to my thoughts to create a cohesive message, which is really good practice for my law class finals coming up this month.

finally, nine's quote that opened this blog is directly followed in 17776 by juice expressing his own thoughts about what lunchables would be like. his story is just as important as everyone else's, so i want to make sure his remarks close out this blog, because i think it gives a good sense of what humans would look like as creatures of play. 

juice, talking about the freedom of lunchables combinations: "ham cheese. ham cracker. cheese cracker. ham and cheese cracker. ham and cheese."
